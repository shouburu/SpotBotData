## What this ranks

“Weirdness” here means how surprising, cumbersome or unnecessarily specific an entry feels in SpotBot’s general-purpose exercise catalog. It is an editorial product judgment based on the current catalog names, equipment and instructions—not a safety, effectiveness, popularity or medical ranking. A specialist event can score highly and still belong in a specialist collection.

**Scope:** catalog-wide name screening, followed by instruction/equipment inspection of the 40 entries ranked below. This is a ranked shortlist, not a claim that the other 819 exercises were individually reviewed or are all suitable. No database entries, reviews, media selections or production rounds were changed.

## Scoring

Each score is the sum of four subjective dimensions: **setup burden (0–3)**, **unusual movement/sequence (0–3)**, **over-specific identity (0–2)** and **opaque or specialist identity (0–2)**. Maximum: 10. Ties use editorial judgment; one-point differences are not meaningful measurements. Scores reflect this app’s intended simplicity, not unfamiliarity alone. Yoga names, accessibility adaptations and supported beginner exercises are not penalized merely for being different from gym lifting.

**Suggested actions:** “Remove candidate” means consider omitting it from the general catalog, not that it has already been deleted. “Merge candidate” requires checking the surviving movement and tracking convention. “Simplify family” moves setup options into guidance while retaining a clear base movement. “Specialist only” means keep out of the default general list if retained. “Split combination” means offer the components separately rather than silently changing what one repetition means.

## Your head-on-bench example

**Rank 12, 7/10.** The instructions really do require forehead contact with an incline bench; this is not just an accidentally verbose title. The support is an actual setup difference, but my product recommendation is to treat it as a rear-delt-raise setup option rather than a standalone discovery item.

The catalog also has Seated Bent-Over Rear Delt Raise, Flat-Bench Chest-Supported Rear Delt Raise, Low-Incline Chest-Supported Rear Delt Raise and Reverse Flyes. Those are related, not automatically interchangeable: standing, seated and chest-supported setups differ. First choose the rear-delt family you want to expose, then preserve any useful setup instructions as options. Do not simply rename every member identically or reassign approved videos without inspecting them.

## Practical cleanup order

1. **Review improvised setups first:** London Bridges, Squat with Plate Movers, rolling-EZ-bar Bodyweight Flyes, Secured Hex-Dumbbell Calf Raise, and Push-Up off of a Dumbbell. These require disproportionately specific explanations and equipment arrangements.
2. **Simplify setup-heavy families:** the head-supported rear-delt raise, crossover-leg floor presses and calf-machine shrug. Distinguish a removable catalog variant from an actual different exercise before merging.
3. **Separate specialist coverage:** strongman implements, open-palm catches and advanced jump combinations. Keep them only if that audience is intentionally supported; avoid treating them as generic substitutes for ordinary lifts.
4. **Rename opaque labels:** Spell Caster, Car Drivers and Anti-Gravity Press deserve plain movement descriptions if retained.
5. **Review combined repetitions separately:** Gorilla Chin/Crunch and Decline Bench Press to Skullcrusher cannot be merged into one component without changing the repetition definition.
