## What the process taught us

- Choose the **teaching question** before the medium. On a constrained machine, setup, depth and seat height may be clearer in two stills than in a generated movement loop. Do not assume every exercise needs video.
- Graphic 3D is the preferred look: ivory volumetric mannequin, navy kit, restrained cel shading, quiet background. Preserve the user's explicit per-asset exceptions. No stick figures or guessed anatomical markers.
- Reference appearance alone does not fix motion. Matching a known bad pose can preserve its error through repeated image edits. A fresh composition can help but can also introduce unwanted text; inspect the actual image before spending on motion.
- Starting from a reviewed missing endpoint helped Lat and Squat achieve the needed range. However, the user's Squat feedback shows a **natural exercise starting position is preferable for delivery**. Separate a technical generation anchor from the eventual viewer-facing sequence.
- Generated eight-second timing is unreliable. Useful cycles often finish early and add another pulse. Review actual phase frames, then author a shorter original-speed delivery with exact provenance. Trimming cannot supply absent depth or correct an unclear elbow.
- AI editorial “candidate” is not user acceptance: Leg Press passed AI review but the user rejected its depth. Preserve both records, and revise the teaching criteria from the user review.
- Flow model offers and CLI help do not establish support by the active frontend. Installed gflow 0.72.0 supports single-start Omni here; end frames are refused on migrated Flow. Do not repeat that diagnostic or force the legacy host.
- Prefer fewer focused generations. Track quote/settlement, rejected takes and remaining limits durably. An unknown failed request is not free; the one zero-cost pre-submit exception has exact proof.
