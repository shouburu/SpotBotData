Round9 production lessons: review a physical reference before paying for motion, then write the motion brief for the pose the image actually shows. A valid low-to-high-to-low cycle may use a different starting phase from the catalog prose; disclose that phase and still require the full range and return. References anchor equipment and appearance but do not guarantee correct movement. Do not impose extra mechanical constraints absent from the catalog, such as locking a pullover dumbbell vertically in world space or requiring every part of a wrist-curl forearm to touch the thigh. Require stable functional support and actual grip continuity.

Frame the complete working movement. Full-body framing matters for standing lifts and changes of stance; a closer supported upper-body or wrist view can omit irrelevant feet while keeping working joints and contacts clear. Prefer a new wider or taller reference over hoping the video will invent missing space. The current Flow account exposes six-, eight- and ten-second Omni settings. Live observations on2026-09-22UTC quoted10,12 and15credits respectively; each submission still needs a fresh exact quote. Use six seconds for small isolated movements where a full cycle fits, eight as the default, and an explicitly authored ten-second sequence for longer lifts. A stale model-pane read initially hid the ten-second option; the bounded model readback guard now waits for the requested picker label before reading its duration and price.

Retain a genuinely correct complete cycle when it can be trimmed from a longer output; add only still endpoint holds, never reverse or invent a return. A verified local color-only mask may remove a misplaced muscle cue from otherwise valid motion, as demonstrated by the Round9 EZ Curl. Preserve the original, identify the edit in the caption, retain source/mask/filter hashes, and inspect the exact derivative across the full cycle. Do not add guessed anatomy or use a mask that detaches from the working muscle. AI screening remains separate from human acceptance and trainer review.

When the same motion substitution repeats, change the physical starting evidence rather than merely lengthening a negative prompt. Current experiments use an opposite endpoint (raised lateral arms, contracted rows, low hip hinge) or a close wrist view excluding the elbow. These remain hypotheses until the exact output passes review. A lunge must preserve which foot steps forward or backward; a full-looking cycle of the wrong foot sequence is still incorrect.

A reviewable repetition requires visible completion at both endpoints. Inspect the actual delivered trim and its final frame; a trim ending just before floor contact or full arm return is not complete. Preserve a valid original-speed cycle when available and disclose minor nonworking appearance imperfections rather than discarding otherwise clear teaching media.

Round9 six-second trials often reduce extra repetitions, but they do not guarantee isolation: three barbell/cable wrist clips completed one cycle while lifting the forearms. Internal authored pose diagrams may clarify a repeatedly misunderstood source geometry (for example, sternum-to-pad contact or forefoot-to-ledge contact). They are construction references only and must never appear as the user's exercise animation. Preserve the Anatomical 3D appearance in the rendered asset. Review the actual result: attaching a diagram is not evidence that its geometry was followed. If a multi-reference edit appears ignored, first inspect the retained upload IDs and reference count before attributing the failure to tooling. Guide-first ordering is an unproven alternative, not a guaranteed correction.

An intermediate starting pose can be used only with a truthful setup phase and an independently complete repetition afterward. Deliver a genuine contiguous full-cycle trim if it exists; reject an output that never reaches both full endpoints. Small harmless visual limitations should be disclosed without inventing form defects, while functional contact, joint motion, the working limb and exercise identity remain hard requirements.

For each new image-to-video shot, inspect the exact source frame first. A correct machine name in a prompt is insufficient: the visible pad, lever, cable route, foot support, bench angle and hand contact must already be plausible. Source-image editing is useful for a specific geometric correction, but do not pay for video until the edited bytes pass this inspection. Reject a deep knee bend labeled stiff-legged, a Smith calf setup with full feet on the step, a press-like fly pose, or a pulley that is disconnected or pulls in the wrong direction.

Write motion as a physical start, one full travel endpoint, a brief hold, one continuous return, and an exact restored starting pose. Specify which joints and equipment parts move and which stay fixed. Inspect all six seconds of the delivered file at dense intervals as well as full-resolution phase frames. Visible extra reversals, abbreviated range, springing, a curl substituted for a shrug, a press substituted for a fly or pullover, or a knee-driven calf press are quality holds even when the visual style is attractive. An original-speed contiguous trim may remove an idle tail only after it contains both endpoints and the complete return; still endpoint holds must be disclosed and must not invent motion.

The orange primary-muscle cue must remain visible at the working endpoint and through the return, not merely at the start and finish. If a cue vanishes mid-cycle, record the exact failure and keep the clip out of human Needs Review.
