## Canonical matching notes

### Clean and Jerk — `ex_clean_and_jerk`

The catalog specifies a floor clean, receiving squat, standing and stabilizing the front rack, an upright dip and drive into a **split** jerk, then recovering the feet inline under a stable overhead bar. Power-clean-only, block-start, cycling and immediate clean-to-jerk variants should not silently substitute for this sequence.

The TrainHeroic candidate was inspected at approximately 0 s (bar on floor), 10.74 s (upward pull), 17.90 s (deep receiving squat), 21.48 s (rising with the bar in the front rack), 27.60 s (overhead split), and 32.60 s (overhead standing recovery). Its loaded competition context is a demonstration of the movement, not a recommended training load. The normal-speed timing of the lift should not be inferred from a slow-motion edit. Written Form guidance must still describe setup, controlled practice, recovery and lowering.

The Catalyst partial reference is valuable for close study of the receive and split. It fails the complete-sequence preference because the sampled near-final frame still has the feet split. The similar-sounding [Clean-Jerk library item](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jiYPejuiNck) was not accepted merely because search labels it 55 seconds: the movement variant and complete sequence were not visually verified. The longer [Clean & Jerk library tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bNCXgyosXlc) was listed as 2:38 in YouTube search and does not meet the requested short-companion target.

### Turkish Get-up — `ex_turkish_get_up`

The catalog specifies a same-side bent knee beneath the raised bell, opposite elbow then hand support, a leg sweep beneath the trunk, half-kneeling, standing, and reversing those stages to the floor. A rise and return is one repetition on the selected arm; both sides are trained separately. The text also calls for securing and lowering the bell with both hands.

Rebecca Rouse's candidate was visually sampled at 0 s (side-lying setup), about 4.96 s (supine with the bell raised and one knee bent), 7.36 s (hand-supported seated phase), 12.36 s (half-kneeling), 14.88 s (standing), 17.36 s (return half-kneeling), 22.36 s (seated return), and 24.38 s (fully supine, bell still raised). Those inspected phases support a complete rise-and-return match on one side. The final two-hand lowering step remains written guidance. No exact anatomical left/right is asserted from a possibly mirrored recording.

The Equinox alternative was inspected at about 11 s (half-kneeling), 13.78 s (standing), and 23 s (return to supine). Its plain background gives good silhouette separation. Portrait preference should not justify cropping a landscape reference or hiding bell, support hand or feet.

## Dead Bug: visible movement and camera guidance

Canonical ID: `cat_cal_dead_bug`. The exercise describes extending **one arm and the opposite leg**, returning them, then alternating. Each diagonal extension-and-return counts as one repetition across both sides.

The supplied video shows a woman on a dark mat against a light wall. The camera is fixed at an elevated oblique side angle, with her head toward screen right and feet toward screen left. Both arms begin raised; hips and knees begin bent. The camera shows the working limbs and the stationary tabletop limbs together, although one side can partly obscure the other.

Inspected phases:

- At the opening, both hands are above the chest/shoulders and both knees are bent over the hips.
- Around 4.52 s, one arm reaches behind the head while the opposite leg straightens and lowers. The other arm stays raised and the other knee stays bent.
- Around 9.52 s, the opposite diagonal is extended. This is alternating contralateral motion, not both legs extending or both arms reaching together.
- Around 15 s, the first diagonal is extended again.
- By 19.52 s, both arms and both bent legs have returned to the starting position.

These are sampled frames and observed transitions, not an exact audited repetition count. The external text gives per-side dosage; the catalog counts total diagonal repetitions, so do not import that dosage into SpotBot.

Prompt implications below are design recommendations derived from the viewed sequence:

1. Keep one clear cycle per diagonal: opposite arm and leg extend together; return completely to tabletop; then repeat on the other diagonal. Hold the nonmoving arm up and nonmoving knee bent throughout the extension.
2. Keep head, shoulders and trunk position visually stable on the mat. Show a comfortable controlled reach rather than maximizing limb travel.
3. Preserve the readable oblique side perspective, but leave visible margin around the fully extended hands and feet. A portrait camera can look diagonally along the body instead of forcing the whole body horizontally across a narrow crop. A landscape source is also acceptable when it communicates the motion better.
4. Use a human or anatomically legible solid figure. Avoid stick figures and speculative markers. Add an anatomical guide only when its position is verifiable and the cue adds useful information.
5. Allow enough time to show extension, return, second diagonal and second return. A proposed 12–16 second two-diagonal teaching loop is a production choice, not the measured timing of the original. If a model cannot preserve that complete sequence in one generation, split the attempt into reviewable shots rather than accepting missing phases.
